  --- lesson ---
A vector can be split into two perpendicular components, one lying along the horizontal axis and one along the vertical axis [1].  
Because the components form a right‑angled triangle with the original vector, trigonometry gives the magnitudes: the horizontal component equals the total magnitude multiplied by the cosine of the angle ( F cos θ ) and the vertical component equals the magnitude multiplied by the sine of the angle ( F sin θ ) [1].  These two perpendicular components can be recombined with the Pythagorean theorem to recover the original vector’s size, just as two perpendicular forces are added by that theorem rather than by simple arithmetic [3].

**Worked example** – A force of 10 N acts 30° above the horizontal.  
Horizontal component = 10 cos 30° ≈ 8.66 N; vertical component = 10 sin 30° = 5.0 N.  
Using the Pythagorean theorem, √(8.66² + 5.0²) ≈ 10 N, confirming that the components correctly represent the original vector [3].

The single mistake most students make is choosing the wrong trigonometric function for a component—using sin for the horizontal or cos for the vertical—so the components are swapped and the result is incorrect [1].
