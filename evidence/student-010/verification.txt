The shape: **batch_id decides what is OFFERED, course_id is the ACTIVE
subject**, and course_id is what every scoped route already read — retrieval,
diagnostic, gaps, mastery, tutor, and every teacher panel. So one switcher
moves an entire surface, with no per-panel changes.

  cohort 6: CSW2 (sem None) + DLD (sem 3)
  fresh student: course_id None batch_id None
  cohorts offered to the student: [(6, 2)]

  teacher switched to: CSW2 | course_id now 5
  heatmap followed the switch: class_size 27 items 6
  teacher switch to an unassigned subject (CS-C) -> 403

The two lines that matter are the ones proving the switch *moves* something:
`/student/course-summary` returned a different subject after the student
switched, and `/teacher/misconceptions/heatmap` answered for the newly
selected subject after the teacher switched. Both refusals are 403, not a
silent no-op — an unchecked switcher would be a way to read another cohort's
material (or a colleague's class) by editing a number.

=== 4. Seed fixes this exposed ===========================================
* **The seeded teacher had a course_id but no assignment row.** The console
  lists what `course_teachers` says (admin-009), so Ravi saw an empty
  switcher and could not move at all. seed.py now creates the assignment for
  any seeded teacher with a course.
* **There was no cohort to enrol in.** A fresh student reached onboarding and
  found an empty list. seed.py now creates the demo cohort (BTech CSE
  2026–2030) and enrols the seeded students in it.
* **The cohort must not contain the prerequisite course.** CSW1 has no corpus
  by design — it is what gap attribution NAMES, not a subject the class is
  taking. Left in, it sorted first and became the default subject for every
  new student: an empty dashboard one click from signup. The seed now takes
  only subjects with a real corpus, and **prunes** ones that no longer
  qualify so a rule change converges instead of leaving yesterday's links.
* **Candidates come from the database, not the seed file.** CS-C was ingested
  by an earlier run and never appears in this seed's data files, so a
  seed-map-only rule silently produced a one-subject cohort. Final state:
  BTech CSE 2026–2030 with CS-C and CSW2, both carrying real corpora.

=== 5. Tests added (backend/tests/test_enrollment.py, 15) ================
  enrolling sets the cohort and defaults to the earliest-semester subject
  enrolling honours an explicit subject
  a batch with no subjects is refused, and nothing is written on that path
  a subject from another cohort cannot be chosen at enrolment
  the enrol path touches no Gap / Mastery / PracticeSet / Attempt row
  subjects list in curriculum order with the active one flagged
  a student who has not enrolled gets an empty list, not an error
  switching within the cohort moves the surface and keeps the cohort
  switching outside the cohort is 403 and does not happen
  a student with no cohort cannot switch at all
  a teacher sees assigned subjects with the cohorts that take them
  a teacher switches only to a subject they are assigned to
  a teacher cannot switch to a colleague's subject
  both teacher routes are teacher-only
  UserOut carries batch_id

=== What verifying found that reading would not have =====================
- **Deleting a batch hit users.batch_id's foreign key**, so a "clean re-seed"
  silently kept the old links and my new filter — which only ADDED — never
  removed CSW1. That is why seed_batch now prunes: a seed that only appends
  cannot fix a rule it got wrong.
- **A default that lands on an empty subject looks like a broken build.**
  Ordering was correct (semester, then code); the data was wrong. Only trying
  the flow as a new user showed it.
