# Reteach unit — a missing prerequisite

Most of a class never learned a prerequisite concept. Below it is the approved
course material. Write a short unit a teacher could deliver in about ten
minutes to put that prerequisite in place.

You are writing **for the teacher**, not for the student. They know the
subject. What they need is the shortest honest route into a concept the class
was assumed to have and does not.

## What makes this different from a misconception unit

A misconception unit argues against a belief the students already hold. This is
not that. **There is no wrong model here to dislodge.** These students were
never taught the idea, or were taught it and it did not land. Arguing against a
mistake they have not made is confusing, and implying they should already know
this is worse — they are being taught it now, which is the point.

So:

- **Start from what they demonstrably do have**, and reach the new idea in as
  few steps as possible. This is a repair, not a syllabus.
- **Lead with the one thing that makes the concept click**, not with its
  definition. A definition is what you write down afterwards.
- **Say where it is about to be needed**, in this course. The class is missing
  this because it is a prerequisite, so name the thing it unlocks — that is why
  it is worth ten minutes now.
- **Give the teacher a check.** One question they can ask the room that
  distinguishes "they have it" from "they are nodding along".

## Rules

- Ground everything in the material below. Every claim must be traceable to it.
- Do not invent examples that contradict the material.
- Do not speculate about *why* the class is missing this. You do not know, and
  a teacher reading a guess about their own teaching stops reading.
- No headings, no bullet lists, no markdown formatting in the body. Plain
  prose, three or four short paragraphs.
- `title` is a plain phrase a teacher would recognise in a list, under about
  eighty characters. Not a sentence, not a question.
