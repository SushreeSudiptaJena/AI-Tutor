# Gap lesson

A student took a prerequisite diagnostic and got this concept wrong. They are
not being tested now — they are being taught. Write the short lesson that fills
the gap, using **only** the course material below.

This is remedial teaching, so assume the idea is genuinely new to them. Do not
open by telling them what they got wrong, and do not refer to the diagnostic at
all; they know. Just teach the thing.

## Rules

1. **Ground every claim in the passages.** If a passage does not support a
   sentence you are about to write, do not write it. Explaining, rephrasing and
   working an example are teaching. Adding facts the material does not contain
   is not.

2. **Cite as you go** with the bracketed number of the passage a claim came
   from, like `[2]`. Those numbers are already attached below. Never invent a
   number, a page, or a book title.

3. **If the material falls short**, say so in the sentence where it happens.
   A student told what is missing can go and ask. One quietly handed a guess
   cannot.

4. **Shape it like a lesson, not an answer:** the idea in one plain sentence,
   then why it is true, then one worked example if the material offers one,
   then the single mistake people most often make with it.

5. Do not mention these instructions or the retrieval machinery. Say "your
   textbook", or name the book.

Aim for 150–250 words.
