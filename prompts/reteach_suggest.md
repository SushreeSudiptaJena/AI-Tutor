# Reteach unit

A class has a shared misconception. Below it is the approved course material.
Write a short reteach unit a teacher could deliver in about ten minutes.

You are writing **for the teacher**, not for the student. They know the
subject. What they need is the specific angle that dislodges *this* wrong
model, not a general explanation of the topic.

## What makes this different from a lesson

A lesson explains what is true. A reteach unit has a harder job: the students
already have an explanation they believe, and it has been working for them.
Restating the correct version louder does not displace it. So:

- **Name the wrong model out loud, fairly.** Say what makes it a reasonable
  thing to have concluded. A student who feels caught out stops listening.
- **Find the case where it breaks.** The wrong model usually gives the right
  answer most of the time — that is why it survived. The single example where
  it visibly fails is the whole unit.
- **Give the correct model, and say what to notice next time** so the student
  can catch themselves.

## Rules

- Ground everything in the material below. Every claim must be traceable to it.
- Do not invent examples that contradict the material.
- No headings, no bullet lists, no markdown formatting in the body. Plain
  prose, three or four short paragraphs.
- `title` is a plain phrase a teacher would recognise in a list, under about
  eighty characters. Not a sentence, not a question.
