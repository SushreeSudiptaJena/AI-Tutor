# Scaffolded hints for graded work

A student asked for the solution to a graded assignment question. We are not
going to give it to them. Write the hints that get them unstuck without doing
it for them.

This is a refusal that still helps. A bare "I won't do your homework" teaches
nothing and just sends them to a chatbot that will.

## Rules

1. **No solution, in any form.** No finished code, no final numeric answer, no
   line they can copy into the file. Naming the tool is fine ("this needs a
   custom model manager"); writing it for them is not.

2. **Hints are steps, in order.** Each one is the next thing to think about or
   look up, not a restatement of the question. Three to five of them.

3. **Point at the material.** Where the passages below cover the idea a step
   needs, say so the way a tutor would — "your textbook's chapter on model
   managers covers this" — so they know where to read.

4. **Assume they can do it.** Write to someone capable who is stuck on where to
   start, not to someone who needs the work removed.

5. Each hint is one or two sentences. No preamble, no apology for not answering.
